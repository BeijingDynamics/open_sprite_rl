# OpenSprite engineering handoff: from reference motion to stable walking

[中文笔记](SPRITE0825_STABLE_WALK_HANDOFF.md)

Recorded 2026-11-09. This note identifies the working baseline before reverse-walking research, explains how it was reached, and records the lessons worth carrying forward. It is not authorization to operate the robot.

## Read this first

**The successful baseline is Sprite0825 v5 with four J4340P shoulder axes, G74 `model3000`, raw six-axis ICM45686 at 800 Hz, a native 50 Hz actor, and native 500 Hz ankle torque control.** The policy was trained in Isaac Lab using velocity-command PPO with an unconditional AMP style prior. The earlier BeyondMimic-style whole-body tracker was used for motion and sim-to-sim validation, not as the actor on the robot. Later SuperMimic experiments did not replace this baseline.

- **Physical evidence:** On 2026-10-16, the same policy and gains completed three supervised, battery-powered 40-second stand–walk–stop trials with fall arrest. The forward command was `0.10 m/s`, not an instrumented ground-speed measurement. No CAN control deadline misses were reported and all 31 motors were verified disabled at exit. The operator reported less left-knee straightening, straighter walking, and stable starts and stops. These are visual findings, not measured foot clearance or absolute heading.
- **Gamepad scope:** On 2026-10-19 the same baseline was confirmed to support Xbox forward, stop and left/right turn. The 120-second entry point allowed `0..0.10 m/s` forward and `±0.10 rad/s` yaw; powered reverse was not released. An offline Bluetooth disconnect check does not establish wireless emergency-stop performance while moving.
- **Pin the asset, not just the name “v5”.** The frozen training URDF has a `2.07092842025253 kg` `waist_yaw_link`. The later `3.7391 kg` model is different. Do not substitute it and call the result a reproduction. The physical success with a battery and Jetson fitted does not establish that the older URDF mass is accurate.
- **A public package is not the field installation.** [G74 model3000](../baselines/sprite0825_stage2_g74_model3000/README.md) is now the default. G74 `model5999` is a retained Isaac candidate; G60 `model3450` is an older sim-to-sim baseline; G59 `model2999` is an earlier native 50 Hz simulation fallback. Field use needs its own calibration, generated native parameters, and libraries. A fresh clone is not ready to energize motors.

## 1. The last stable walking recipe

1. **Stage 1: reference-conditioned whole-body tracking (WBT).** Reference joint states and relative poses command the tracker through a clip. This checks whether the motion can be realized with reasonable contact and load. Sprite0615 V38 was a key sim-to-sim baseline tested across speeds, phases, perturbations and long loops. Kinematic playback, closed-loop WBT, and gamepad locomotion without a reference are distinct.
2. **Stage 2: velocity control with AMP.** PPO learns standing, velocity tracking, turning, contact and efficiency from task rewards. AMP regularizes the motion distribution without specifying the current speed or the foot position of a particular reference frame. G74 was trained from scratch with `UnconditionedAMPPPO`, following G72/G73 and using G71-derived expert data. It was neither distilled from the V38 actor nor trained with a world model. The division between command and style follows published PM01 ideas, not a claim to reproduce the manufacturer's full commercial pipeline.

## 2. GMR and retargeting: what went wrong and what worked

### Why the early experiments went in circles

- **IK cannot turn exaggerated performance motion into ordinary walking.** Two early candidates were performance clips. Start with steady, natural flat-ground motion. Early GMR `walk_6m` often reached joint limits; the operator preferred `bmlrub112_walk1/2`, but both ankle pitch joints were still near their upper limits. Visual screening alone was insufficient.
- **Measure the foot chain against the floor.** Flat-looking feet and plausible knees do not establish absence of penetration or sliding. Check target FK and actual sole collision geometry, not just ankle origins. A global height offset is not a contact correction. The early verbal “45” is not a general height parameter. Check inherited collision masks too: a later experiment was blocked at roughly 4.6 cm penetration, which should not be admitted by raising the clip or weakening the gate.
- **Pose alignment is not phase alignment.** An initial 30-frame offset aligned poses but not both walk clips; windows near frames 16 and 79 fit walk1 and walk2 better, respectively. A mistimed knee anchor can amplify an early step on one side. Reference playback demonstrated that the knee could flex, so mechanical restriction was not the whole explanation. A weak extra loss did not change gait; strong action anchoring or a high learning rate could degrade contact.
- **Spatial scaling does not solve joint speed.** Sprite0615 WBT `timescale150` lengthened the motion to 1.5 times its duration, making it about two-thirds as fast. It was not a gain multiplier. The operator chose 1.5 over 1.75 for that motion and actuator envelope. Do not reapply it to G71/G74.
- **A recurring jump is not necessarily a push.** Log external forces, resets, loop boundaries and initialization separately. Long closed-loop tests without reset, fixed initial conditions and timestamped events distinguish recovery from a reset to a favorable pose.

### The final expert was not raw GMR output

The production chain was **steady PM01 G55 walk → source MuJoCo FK → semantic landmarks → Sprite0825 Mink IK → contact cleanup → G71 V20 → AMP clips**. Keep it distinct from the earlier GMR experiments.

1. Match soles, hips, torso, shoulders and elbows using corresponding geometry. Handle axis signs, nominal poses, scale and missing freedoms by joint name; do not copy joint angles between different robots. G71 used lateral hip points for the tilted hip axes, projected torso targets onto Sprite's rigid geometry, and did not force the arms to track a PM01 elbow-yaw endpoint absent on Sprite.
2. Detect support using heel/toe height and planar velocity. V20 thresholds were `0.025 m` and `0.08 m/s`. Smoothly correct root **XY** using support anchors, retaining joint angles and root orientation. Remove exactly one unconverged initial frame from all synchronized arrays. Recompute derivatives and FK after resampling, cleaning or timing changes.
3. V20 has 1199 frames at 100 Hz (11.99 s), about `0.196 m/s` average speed and 148 steps/min, with 100% alternating contacts. Minimum heel/toe height was `3.69 mm`, worst contact-point planar speed P90 `9.01 mm/s`, with no joint-limit violations. Independent FK over all 32 bodies agreed within about `2.41e-7 m` in position. These are kinematic reference checks, not closed-loop dynamic acceptance.
4. Audit playback across formats: names, axes, units, quaternion and body ordering, nominal pose, soles, root velocity frame, clip seams and alternation. Require both visual and numerical evidence before training. The earlier short tiled V38 cycles were useful for WBT, not for AMP start/stop coverage. Conversely, full PM01 clips can include side motion and turning; filter for the task before adding competing rewards.

**Reusable order:** validate source motion → morphology/axes/FK → floor and limits → contact and seams → expert/command coverage → closed-loop physics. Do not use rewards to compensate for an unvalidated reference.

## 3. The decisions that moved the project forward

### Training and simulation transfer

- **Use reference-constrained whole-body motion before tuning local gait shape.** WBT gave a way to inspect phase, contact and movement quality. AMP supplied a distribution without forcing the command actor to replay a clip on a fixed clock. Early knee and step-length rewards could improve isolated metrics while harming yaw and contact.
- **Keep rated torque, peak torque, speed, and wire encoding separate.** J4340P's `14/40 Nm` and roughly `3.77 rad/s` rated speed are not a hard speed cap; the project used a `9.3 rad/s` modeled operating limit around 38 V. The J4310P ankles are different. Screen peak and torque-speed limits as well as sustained RMS. The modeled envelope is an engineering constraint, not a measured manufacturer dynamic curve or proof of safe continuous peak torque.
- **Train at 50 Hz rather than replaying a 100 Hz actor more slowly.** G58F was native 100 Hz; G59 was scratch-trained at 50 Hz with a new history length. G59 addressed short, fast steps but introduced longer steps and more upper-body sway. G60 rescaled first- and second-order action-difference penalties for their `dt²/dt⁴` dependence and softly constrained waist roll instead of locking it. A curriculum counter reset during continuation was a confound, so preserve the physical duration and resume semantics when reproducing.
- **Fix an offset before adding torque.** After upgrading the four proximal shoulder axes, G73 retained a roughly `0.105 rad` shoulder-roll bias. The inherited `+0.25/-0.25 rad` centers conflicted with the expert. G74 aligned default pose, action offset and posture reward centers to about `+0.082816/-0.076322 rad`, kept the absolute-angle expert unchanged and trained from scratch.
- **Isaac, MuJoCo and the robot are separate gates.** The 4090 made enough parallel experience practical; GPU model alone did not explain the improvement. Compare samples, physics step, seeds and commands, and measure drift, both turns, repeated starts/stops, contact, bilateral swing, slip and actuator loads. This is why model 3000 replaced 5999 as the preferred checkpoint. Use simulation time, not wall time, for cadence when visualization runs below real time.

### Actuation and physical validation

- **The policy uses serial joint coordinates; the physical parallel mechanism is handled in runtime.** Calibrate both ankles independently rather than copying a mirrored side. Head pitch/roll has its own 2×2 map and yaw is independent. For `q_m = A q_j + b`, recover joint positions with `A⁻¹(q_m-b)`, omit the offset for velocity, and send motor torque `τ_m = A⁻ᵀτ_j` to preserve virtual work. Ankle PD uses fresh feedback at 500 Hz and sends MIT zero Kp/Kd with feedforward torque. No foot-wrench round trip is needed. The head instead retains 50 Hz position mapping, internal PD and cross feedforward. A linear map is only trustworthy inside its calibrated region; loosening a residual gate does not cure nonlinear geometry.
- **Separate encoder, model and motor signs.** Keep `encoder_sign` as installed. Use `policy_to_motor_sign` for waist yaw, left wrist pitch/roll, right wrist roll and head yaw. Test both directions of the mapping and transformed limits. Correct coupled pitch in the matrix column, not by reversing one motor. Fitted CSV offsets are not commands to reset drive zeros. Correcting one head motor-B sign reduced RMS from about `0.034` to `0.0011 rad`; inspect the largest residual before changing the model.
- **Protocol ranges, mechanical ratings and operational limits differ.** Read back MIT PMAX/VMAX/TMAX. The J4340P wire TMAX of `28 Nm` is distinct from `14/40 Nm` rated/peak; J4310P's `10 Nm` wire range is distinct from `3.5/12.5 Nm`. Check total `Kp·position_error + Kd·velocity_error + τff`, post-quantization results, and feedback anomalies. Use filtered temperatures and torque RMS for sustained load. Count projection, mechanical-limit contact and torque saturation separately.
- **Advance from low-risk observations to closed loop.** Query with zero gains and verify directions, responses and faults on all buses and 31 motors; disabled drives do not necessarily broadcast. Then test single-joint holds, unloaded coupled pairs, suspended full-body control, grounded load and perturbation, and finally stand–walk–stop. Start from measured pose and ramp within limits. An advisory startup posture check does not replace mechanical command bounds, fresh IMU data, timeouts and independent power cutoff. Never automatically write zeros. A 5 A current-limited bench supply caused voltage sag; do not interpret that as policy failure. The walking trials used battery power.
- **Commissioning torque limits are not locomotion torque limits.** A low torque test can establish direction and timing without proving the ability to support weight. Physical comparisons included hip/knee `130/5`, hip roll `150/5`, and waist roll `60/3`; later shoulder and wrist tuning addressed stiffness and fast oscillations. Kd 5 worked for the tested hip/knee setup, not every Damiao drive. Use the recorded final group settings and [ICM field report](https://github.com/BeijingDynamics/open_sprite_runtime/blob/250da14/docs/ICM45686_WALKING_20261016.md). Do not reapply an old multiplier or send training gains straight to the robot.

### IMU, timing and native runtime

- **Preserve observation meaning while reducing delay.** The actor consumes body angular velocity and projected gravity, not quaternions or acceleration. Subtract the gyro bias estimated during 15 s stillness, apply the mount rotation, and keep estimator correction out of the gyro observation. Acceleration aids a software gravity estimate; motion acceleration is not gravity. Integrate using actual sample intervals. Six-axis sensing has no magnetic heading; startup zeroing does not remove long-term drift. Pure yaw error does not directly change projected gravity. Feeding raw specific force into this actor instead would require retraining and validation.
- **The measured progression matters.** Yahboom fused serial output → BNO055 experiment → BNO raw six-axis software estimation (200 Hz host reads) → ICM45686 raw SPI at 800 Hz with DRDY timestamps. BNO raw already improved straightness in three walks. The ICM also passed three trials with the same policy and gains. The results support improving the sensing path, but do not isolate ODR, filters, interface, bias or garbage collection as the sole cause.
- **Do not block control on inference or I/O.** Independent native IMU production, CPU ONNX Runtime and the CAN/ankle controller use latest-state snapshots, bounded IPC, monotonic timestamps and stale-data rejection. The control thread does not wait for inference, gamepad or disk. The CAN scheduler ticks at **2 kHz**, the four ankle motors at **500 Hz each**, the remaining 27 at **50 Hz each**, the actor at **50 Hz**, and the motor internal MIT/current loop at **1 kHz**. The 2 kHz figure is not per-motor traffic. Hold each 50 Hz target until the next update.
- **Distinguish age from intrinsic sensor delay.** A 120 s zero-gain ICM shadow produced 6000 policy frames, byte-identical online/offline outputs, about 792.2 Hz accepted samples and a roughly 2.53 ms maximum sample gap. Policy-consumption age was p99 6.57 ms and max 7.34 ms; inference max was about 1.85 ms. The age begins at DRDY and does not capture sensor/filter latency before the edge. Tail delays matter more than average rate. Native code removed Python GC from the critical path but not all kernel scheduling tails.
- **Remember the SPI failure.** Read WHO_AM_I as `F2 00` in one 16-clock transaction with CS held low. Verify SCK on the bare header and with loopback before probing at the module. Four series resistors had been mistaken for pull-ups and removed, opening the SPI connections. Restoring the circuit fixed the read; byte order did not. The walking setup used mode 3, requested 1 MHz SPI, 800 Hz ODR and INT1. Requested SPI rate is not a scope measurement. Bounded runtime-PM settings addressed tails and were restored on exit. The 6400 Hz FIFO experiment was isolated and not used for walking.

## 4. What reverse walking and SuperMimic did not solve

- G74 never trained on negative forward velocity and has no qualified reverse expert. Allowing negative gamepad commands does not implement reverse. Backward drift, staying upright and input/IPC parity do not qualify it. Changing the model, IMU observation, references and rewards together makes failures hard to attribute.
- Preserve representative forward regressions and reverse failure traces, not every run. At 50 Hz a contact-slip percentile depends on sampling phase; high-rate contact integration and a low-rate gate are different evidence. No reverse model passed physical acceptance, so none replaced the working version.
- SuperMimic was a separate branch. Align robot, serial PD/limits and the reproduced tracker recipe first; do not mix measured parallel mappings, MIT quantization or runtime guards into initial training. The original G74 used serial state/action and an **ideal** ankle torque-speed envelope, not the measured runtime map. In later experiments, the 17,175-update checkpoint tracked speed better in a long test but stopped worse; the 24,000-update checkpoint backed up and turned at zero command. Velocity critic loss rose from around update 21,280 and the root cause was not settled. Survival alone missed the failure. Keep the intermediate evidence; do not deploy the final export.

## 5. How to restart in another project

1. **Take this note and the minimum baseline.** Restore model 3000, its resolved training parameters, matching URDF/USD/MJCF, G71 data, deployment contract, runtime commit and build inputs, field calibration and the three trial reports. The public repository does not ship raw robot logs or site calibration. List any missing input explicitly. Historical results do not attest to today's robot.
2. **Recover offline before training or enabling anything.** Check hashes, 31 joint names, action scale/offset, history order, mount rotation, ankle/head mappings, rates and command bounds. Replay G74 standing, forward, both turns and repeated stops/starts; compare speed, stopping, sole contact, saturation and torque RMS. Use recorded inputs for ONNX/native parity and simulated fault tests. Hardware shadow is a later onsite step.
3. **Treat reverse as a separate capability.** Audit low-speed reverse and transitions, verify actual command/reference sampling, and change one testable factor at a time. Preserve the forward, turn and stop baselines. Require negative ground displacement and appropriate contact, not just survival. Choose a checkpoint on long task tests, not its iteration number.
4. **Get separate onsite authorization for powered work.** Verify battery, fall arrest, clear space, safety operator and independent cutoff. Check the actual hardware configuration before staged tests. On failure retain logs; do not hide it by renaming a build, resetting zeros or widening limits.

Suggested first task: **“Restore and verify G74 model3000 + ICM800 offline as the known forward/turn baseline. Use the frozen package and actual configuration hashes, leave the robot and runtime untouched, and report evidence and gaps before discussing reverse or a different training method.”**
